On May 24, 1889, the Governor-General of Indochina issued a decree establishing the city of Đà Nẵng

(known as Tourane).

From 1888 to 1945, the French gradually transformed Da Nang into a modern Western-style city. The urban

planning centered around the Han River, with Quai Courbet Street (Bach Dang Street today) along the Han

River as the main axis and expanded to the west to establish grid street systems. The city was divided into

two quarters, French and Vietnamese. In 1889, the urban area included 5 communes, and in 1901, it

expanded to 19 communes. Numerous buildings were constructed, such as the Governor's Palace,

Customs Office, Post Office, the Museum of Cham Culture, Da Nang Cathedral, etc...; simultaneously, the

city’s airport, seaport, and railway system were established and upgraded. Various kinds of new economic

activities emerged. The French imported Western lifestyles into Da Nang, influencing the lifestyle and

dressing of some of its residents. In July 1923, Da Nang people began using lighting,

and the city became

the busiest in Central Vietnam.
